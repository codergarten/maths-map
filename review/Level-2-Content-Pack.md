## Level 2 achievement standard (the "definition of done")

> By the end of Level 2, students order and represent numbers to at least 1000; apply knowledge of place value to partition, rearrange and rename two- and three-digit numbers in terms of their parts; and regroup partitioned numbers to assist in calculations. They use mathematical modelling to solve practical additive and multiplicative problems, including money transactions, representing the situation and choosing calculation strategies. Students identify and represent part-whole relationships of halves, quarters and eighths in measurement contexts. Students describe and continue patterns that increase and decrease additively by a constant amount and identify missing elements in the pattern. They recall and demonstrate proficiency with addition and subtraction facts within 20 and multiplication facts for twos. Students use uniform informal units to measure and compare shapes and objects. They determine the number of days between events using a calendar and read time on an analog clock to the hour, half-hour and quarter hour. Students use quarter, half, three-quarter and full measures of turn in everyday situations. Students compare and classify shapes, describing features using formal spatial terms. They locate and identify positions of features in two-dimensional representations and move position by following directions and pathways. Students use a range of methods to collect, record, represent and interpret categorical data in response to questions.

## Number strand (6 codes)

### VC2M2N01 — Recognise, represent and order numbers to 1000
*Plain words:* ordering up to 1000, including digits with zeros (808/880/818/881) and missing-number lines.
*Items:* L2-N01-1 (after 899 → 900) · L2-N01-2 (order 610/106/400) · L2-N01-3 (385 vs 358) · L2-N01-4 (smallest of 808/880/818/881 → 808) · L2-N01-5 (observe: order 5 three-digit cards with place-value explanation)
*Activities:* hundreds-chart puzzles · broken number line (1800–2200) · recycling bottle-top bundles into ones/tens/hundreds

### VC2M2N02 — Partition, rearrange, regroup, rename two- and three-digit numbers; role of the zero digit
*Items:* L2-N02-1 (245 = 2h 4t 5o) · L2-N02-2 (307's zero = no tens) · L2-N02-3 (rename 245 as 24 tens and 5 ones) · L2-N02-4 (35 ones + 2 hundreds = 235) · L2-N02-5 (observe: rename 326 three ways)
*Activities:* rename-race drills · craft-stick bundles (326 as 3-2-6 / 32t+6 / 3h+26) · zero detective (307 vs 370 vs 703)

### VC2M2N03 — One-half, quarters, eighths; repeated halving
*Items:* L2-N03-1 (quarter of pizza) · L2-N03-2 (half of 12 → 6) · L2-N03-3 (1/2 > 1/4) · L2-N03-4 (halve then halve → 4 parts) · L2-N03-5 (observe: repeated halving strips)
*Activities:* paper-strip halving ladder · washers-vs-bolts sharing · pizza fraction Fridays

### VC2M2N04 — Add and subtract 1–2 digit numbers; number sentences; part-part-whole
*Items:* L2-N04-1 (7+8) · L2-N04-2 (56+37 → 93, jump the tens/ones) · L2-N04-3 (gift-card $100−$38 → $62) · L2-N04-4 (45+20) · L2-N04-5 (observe: $100 card, shoes+tee, number sentence)
*Activities:* near-doubles via beanbag toss · gift-card problem variants · "say the sum in parts" (56+37 = 80+13)

### VC2M2N05 — Multiply/divide by one-digit numbers; repeated addition, equal grouping, arrays, partitioning
*Items:* L2-N05-1 (4 trays × 6 → 24) · L2-N05-2 (3 fives array → 15) · L2-N05-3 ($36 ÷ 4 → $9) · L2-N05-4 (which is NOT 12: 5×2) · L2-N05-5 (observe: 24 counters — 4 plates vs groups of 4)
*Activities:* array hunt (all 12-arrays) · $36 prize problem · Think Boards

### VC2M2N06 — Mathematical modelling: additive/multiplicative incl. money; choose operation; interpret solutions
*Items:* L2-N06-1 (25 kids × $2 — which operation? multiply) · L2-N06-2 (3 books @ $5 → $15) · L2-N06-3 (teams of 4 from 16 → 4) · L2-N06-4 ($50−$23 → $27) · L2-N06-5 (observe: family fundraiser)
*Activities:* play shop with real money · fundraiser problem · team-formation maths
