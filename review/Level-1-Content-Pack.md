# Maths Map — Level 1 Content Pack (for your review)

**Status:** Draft v0.1 — please read and mark up before we build the app around it.
**Source:** Victorian Curriculum F–10 (Version 2.0), Mathematics, Level 1 — © VCAA, CC-BY-NC 3.0.
**What this file contains:** for each of the 15 Level 1 content descriptions — plain-English summary, what to watch for, at-home activities to close gaps, and the draft assessment items tagged to it.

---

## How the app will use this

- Each assessment item is worth **2 points** (secure) or **0**. Parent-observed tasks can also score **1** (partially secure).
- Per content code: **Secure ≥ 80% · Developing 50–79% · Emerging < 50%** (of points earned).
- The gap report groups codes into **Strong** / **Developing** / **Needs work**, and the parent-friendly "how to build this" text below comes straight from the activity lists.
- 75 items total: 59 on-screen taps, 1 ordering task, 15 parent-observed hands-on tasks (~25–35 min for a full sitting).

## Number strand

### VC2M1N01 — Recognise, represent and order numbers to 120
*Curriculum (VCAA):* recognise, represent and order numbers to at least 120 using materials, numerals, number lines and charts.

**In plain words:** your child can read, write and say numbers up to about 120, and knows what "bigger" and "smaller" mean in numbers.

**Why it matters next:** everything in Level 2 (numbers to 1000, place value) is built on this. Weak ordering now = shaky hundreds charts later.

**Watch for:** confusing teen numbers with tens (17 vs 71 — "sixteen and seventy" are easily swapped); counting on from one instead of continuing; treating digits independently (46 > 64 because 6 > 4).

**Activities:**
- **Hundreds chart hunt** — print/draw a 0–99 chart, cover some numbers, ask them to name the missing ones. Build it together from cards first.
- **Number-line hops** — peg clothes-pegged numbered cards on a line; order them by pegging onto a blank line from memory.
- **Skip-count songs in 2s/5s/10s** at breakfast — counting is a rhythm before it's a concept.
- *(VCAA elaboration nod: numbers look/sound similar — 16, 60, 61, 66 — make a "number detective" game of spotting the odd one.)*

**Items:** L1-N01-1 (after 59) · L1-N01-2 (order 12/24/36) · L1-N01-3 (46 vs 64) · L1-N01-4 (35 = 3 tens 5 ones) · L1-N01-5 (observe: count 50 objects by tens)

### VC2M1N02 — Partition one- and two-digit numbers in different ways
*Curriculum (VCAA):* partition one- and two-digit numbers using materials, including partitioning into tens and ones.

**In plain words:** knowing that 8 can be 5+3 or 4+4, and that 35 is 3 tens and 5 ones (or even 2 tens and 15 ones).

**Why it matters next:** part-part-whole to 10 is the engine of mental arithmetic; tens-and-ones is the whole of place value.

**Watch for:** only knowing "counting up" from 1; not seeing that 13+2 and 13+5 both start at 13; struggle to see a decade as "ten ones" glued together.

**Activities:**
- **Ten-frames for fingers** — 5+5 grid; ask how to fill it to make 8 (5+3), to make 6 (5+1). Free ten-frame PDFs or draw on paper.
- **Coins from a moneybox** — sort $1 coins into piles of 10 to "make decades"; count the leftovers.
- **"How many ways to make 10?"** — race with dice to pair numbers that make 10 (7 pairs with 3, etc.).

**Items:** L1-N02-1 (8 = 5 + ?) · L1-N02-2 (24 = 10 + ?) · L1-N02-3 (35 = tens and ones) · L1-N02-4 (7 + ? = 10) · L1-N02-5 (observe: partition 13 two ways)

### VC2M1N03 — Quantify sets to at least 120 by grouping and skip counting
*Curriculum (VCAA):* quantify sets of objects to at least 120 by partitioning into equal groups and skip counting.

**In plain words:** when faced with a big pile, smart kids make groups of 10 (or 5), count "10, 20, 30…" and finish accurately. One-by-one counting of 87 marbles is the failure mode.

**Why it matters next:** multiplication is equal groups; grouping into 10s is the foundation of place-value arithmetic.

**Watch for:** losing which group they're on; skip counting by 10s fine but not by 5s or 2s; not connecting the "3 left over" to the ones digit.

**Activities:**
- **Count the pantry** — ask your child to count pasta/tickle/pegs/$1 coins into piles of 10; you track how many piles they make.
- **Skip-count the house** — "How many toes in this family?" (5s), "wheels on these cars?" (4s — just for fun, not expected), "fingers on all our hands?" (5s or 10s).
- **Hundreds race** — roll a 10-sided die (or pick tens), jump along 10, 20, 30… to 100/120.

**Items:** L1-N03-1 (skip 2s) · L1-N03-2 (skip 5s) · L1-N03-3 (skip 10s) · L1-N03-4 (47 marbles → full groups of 10?) · L1-N03-5 (observe: count 40 objects by tens)

### VC2M1N04 — Add and subtract within 20 using materials and part-part-whole knowledge
*Curriculum (VCAA):* add and subtract within 20 using materials, part-part-whole to 10 and a variety of calculation strategies.

**In plain words:** fluent small additions and subtractions, ideally by strategy (8+6 is 8+2+4) not by counting from 1 every time.

**Why it matters next:** Level 2 mental computation and written strategies rest entirely on within-20 fluency.

**Watch for:** counting on from the wrong number; finger-counting every sum from 1; not using "make 10" (8+5 → 8+2+3); commutativity gaps (6+4 known, 4+6 not).

**Activities:**
- **Ten-frame + fingers** — practice 8+5, 7+6, 13−5 with pictures of ten-frames.
- **Story problems at dinner** — "There were 9 peas on your plate. You ate 4. How many now?" — always ask "how did you work it out?"
- **Number pairs to 10** — shower-thoughts trivia: "What goes with 8 to make 10?"

**Items:** L1-N04-1 (6+4) · L1-N04-2 (13−5) · L1-N04-3 (8+5) · L1-N04-4 (12−4) · L1-N04-5 (observe: two story problems + explain strategy)

### VC2M1N05 — Solve practical additive problems incl. simple money (modelling)
*Curriculum (VCAA):* use mathematical modelling for additive situations incl. simple money transactions; represent with diagrams/materials; use calculation strategies.

**In plain words:** turning a small story ("You had $10, bought a $4 snack, what's left?") into a number sentence and solving it — this is real maths, not worksheet maths.

**Why it matters next:** word problems with money, time and measures all arrive together in Level 2.

**Watch for:** being able to compute 6+4 but freezing when it's wrapped in a story; not knowing what the question is asking ("how many left" vs "how many altogether").

**Activities:**
- **Play shop** — prices $1–$10, $15 play money; your child is shopkeeper and customer on alternate days (elaboration from the PDF).
- **Bus/lift counting game** — "6 people on the bus, 4 get on, 2 get off…" narrate; they report the total each step.
- **Pocket-money ledger** — give them a little "account": $10 on Monday, $2 for a juice — they track what's left on a napkin.

**Items:** L1-N05-1 ($5+$2) · L1-N05-2 (change from $10 for $8 toy) · L1-N05-3 (bus: 6+4) · L1-N05-4 ($10−$4 left) · L1-N05-5 (observe: play shop, two purchases, change/left-over)

### VC2M1N06 — Solve practical problems with equal sharing and grouping (modelling)
*Curriculum (VCAA):* use mathematical modelling for equal sharing and grouping; diagrams, materials; strategies.

**In plain words:** sharing and grouping problems — "3 cars, 4 wheels each, how many wheels?" and "share 15 biscuits between 3 kids."

**Why it matters next:** this IS multiplication and division, in disguise, before they know the words.

**Watch for:** dealing into groups but losing count; not seeing remainders ("but nobody gets the last 2!"); confusion between sharing (division) and grouping.

**Activities:**
- **Deal the cards** — deal a deck equally to 2, 3 or 4 players, counting aloud one-for-each.
- **Wheel counting** — "How many wheels in 4 toy cars?" (draw it!) — seed multiplication with skip counting.
- **Coin sharing** — sorting coins from a moneybox equally to 4 people (straight from the elaboration).

**Items:** L1-N06-1 (3 cars × 4 wheels) · L1-N06-2 (share 10 between 2) · L1-N06-3 (bags of 4 from 12) · L1-N06-4 (share 8 coins between 4) · L1-N06-5 (observe: share 14 between 2; 15 between 3)

## Algebra strand

### VC2M1A01 — Skip-counting pattern sequences (twos, fives, tens)
*Curriculum (VCAA):* recognise, continue and create pattern sequences with skip counting.

**In plain words:** 2, 4, 6, 8… — knowing the rhythm, extending it, spotting where it goes wrong.

**Why it matters next:** multiplication tables (L2), even/odd numbers, place value.

**Watch for:** can recite but can't extend a sequence on paper; mixes up 5s and 10s; thinks "fourteens" when they mean "forty" (teens vs tens confusion).

**Activities:**
- **Egg carton counter** — 10-count empty egg carton: drop in 2 beads at a time, count 2,4,6… to 20.
- **Skip-count hopscotch** — outside numbers chalked in tens; your child hops on 10, 20, 30…
- **Coin piles** — stack $1 coins in fives, count aloud to 50.

**Items:** L1-A01-1 (next after 8 by 2s) · L1-A01-2 (15 → next by 5s) · L1-A01-3 (30 → next by 10s) · L1-A01-4 (last digit when counting by 2s) · L1-A01-5 (observe: build 2,4,6,8 and count by 10s to 100)

---

### VC2M1A02 — Repeating patterns and the unit of repeat
*Curriculum (VCAA):* recognise, continue and create repeating patterns; identify the repeating unit.

**In plain words:** ABAB and ABBABB patterns — knowing what repeats, continuing them, inventing their own.

**Why it matters next:** algebraic thinking — the beginning of seeing structure and generalising.

**Watch for:** copying a pattern mechanically without knowing WHY; losing the phase in longer units; confusing colour with position when the unit is length-based.

**Activities:**
- **Bead necklaces** — pattern red-blue-blue with real beads/pasta/string (elaboration: shell and seed necklaces).
- **Stamp patterns** — draw a stamp-repeat (stamp stamp clap, stamp stamp clap) and continue/perform it.
- **Arranging the bookshelf** — serious business: "big book, small book, small book" repeat and classify.

**Items:** L1-A02-1 (🔴🔵… next) · L1-A02-2 (identify unit in ✦✦★) · L1-A02-3 (2,4,6,9,10 — odd one out) · L1-A02-4 (★◆◆★◆◆… next two) · L1-A02-5 (observe: continue red-blue-blue, name the unit)

## Measurement strand

### VC2M1M01 — Compare and order length, mass, capacity and duration
*Curriculum (VCAA):* compare directly and indirectly and order objects and events using length, mass, capacity and duration; communicate reasoning.

**In plain words:** which is longer, heavier, holds more, takes longer — and being able to SAY how they know.

**Why it matters next:** formal units (cm, kg, mL, hours) in Level 2 need the comparison intuition first.

**Watch for:** "it's bigger" = longer in length but also heavier/fuller (scale confusion); duration vs clock-reading confusion; only comparing pairs, not ordering 3+.

**Activities:**
- **Sand-timer race** — hourglass/homemade sand timer: compare how long each runs (elaboration).
- **Hefting rocks/fruit** — three fruits in order from lightest to heaviest, with balance scales if you have them.
- **Pour-off** — which of 3 cups holds most? Pour one at a time to prove it.
- **The family feet** — compare shoe length by stepping into the kitchen, side-by-side (direct comparison).

**Items:** L1-M01-1 (bathtub vs cup) · L1-M01-2 (rice bag vs feather) · L1-M01-3 (blink vs nap) · L1-M01-4 (8 vs 5 paperclips) · L1-M01-5 (observe: order length/capacity/mass with explanations)

---

### VC2M1M02 — Measure length with informal uniform units, end-to-end
*Curriculum (VCAA):* measure length of shapes and objects using informal units; uniform and end-to-end.

**In plain words:** using paperclips/blocks/shoes to measure, and knowing units must be the SAME size with NO gaps or overlaps.

**Why it matters next:** why we bother with standard units (cm/m) — direct experience of their unfairness comes first.

**Watch for:** gaps and overlaps; different-sized units in one measurement; not knowing the answer is meaningless unless you record units.

**Activities:**
- **"How long is the lounge?"** — measure with shoes, then with a 30cm ruler if you have one; notice the numbers differ and ask why.
- **Block towers vs book** — count how many blocks high the bookshelf is.
- **Two units, same desk** — measure with icy-pole sticks, then pencils; compare counts (elaboration from the PDF).

**Items:** L1-M02-1 (different units → different numbers) · L1-M02-2 (gaps OK? False) · L1-M02-3 (9 vs 6 paperclips) · L1-M02-4 (why same-size units?) · L1-M02-5 (observe: measure table end-to-end + explain gaps)

---

### VC2M1M03 — Duration and sequence of events (years, months, weeks, days, hours)
*Curriculum (VCAA):* describe duration and sequence of events using years, months, weeks, days and hours.

**In plain words:** ordering morning/afternoon/night; naming days and months; knowing hours/days/weeks are different & that a year is long.

**Why it matters next:** telling time to the hour/half-hour, calendar work, durations — all in Level 2.

**Watch for:** days vs months vs years order confusion; "Christmas is in the wrong month" (month cyclicity); knowing the words but not relative scale (a week vs a year).

**Activities:**
- **Pictorial timeline** — draw their life: birth, first steps, kindy, now; glue photos (elaboration).
- **Sand timers (again)** — 1-minute timer: "how long is "no more screen time"?" — lets them FEEL a minute.
- **Calendar marking** — mark birthdays, school terms; count weeks until a holiday.

**Items:** L1-M03-1 (hour vs week vs year) · L1-M03-2 (morning/afternoon/night) · L1-M03-3 (Jan/Apr/Aug) · L1-M03-4 (days in week) · L1-M03-5 (observe: days after Tuesday, months, sequencing, time scale)

## Space strand

### VC2M1SP01 — Make, compare and classify familiar shapes and objects
*Curriculum (VCAA):* make, compare and classify familiar shapes and objects; recognise in the environment; identify similarities and differences.

**In plain words:** naming 2-D shapes (circle, oval, triangle, square, rectangle…), spotting them in the real world, sorting them by features (round/straight, sides, corners).

**Why it matters next:** Level 2 introduces parallel/curved/straight language, symmetry, and 3-D names.

**Watch for:** calling every 4-sided shape a square; "oval because it's a squashed circle" (actually fine at this age); not seeing "corners" vs "sides" distinction.

**Activities:**
- **Shape hunt around the house** — find 3 triangles, 3 circles, 3 rectangles (they're EVERYWHERE: clocks, windows, signs).
- **Feely shapes** — shapes in an opaque bag; describe by touch, guess the shape (elaboration).
- **Sorting laundry** — sort socks by colour/texture/size — practises classifying by a RULE generally.

**Items:** L1-SP01-1 (3 sides) · L1-SP01-2 (which is round 🔺🟦⚫) · L1-SP01-3 (corners of a square) · L1-SP01-4 (odd one out) · L1-SP01-5 (observe: sort mixed shapes + find shapes in room)

---

### VC2M1SP02 — Give and follow directions to move within a space
*Curriculum (VCAA):* give and follow directions to move people and objects to different locations.

**In plain words:** left/right/forward/back/straight; giving simple instructions; being the robot.

**Why it matters next:** grid navigation, maps, coordinates — and genuinely useful for Minecraft/Roblox/IRL navigation.

**Watch for:** left/right transposed; "over there" instead of directional language; great at being the robot, hopeless at giving precise directions.

**Activities:**
- **Human robot** — child gives YOU directions from the door to the sofa; you only move as told (elaboration).
- **Treasure hunt** — hide a toy; child writes/draws 4–5 step instructions for you to follow to find it.
- **Block maze** — build a LEGO maze; swap roles: one navigates, one builds.
- **Cardboard city directions** — "walk to the park: turn left at the bakery…" with toy cars.

**Items:** L1-SP02-1 (robot command sequence) · L1-SP02-2 (backwards) · L1-SP02-3 ("left/right" = directions) · L1-SP02-4 (three forward one back) · L1-SP02-5 (observe: directing you then following directions)

## Statistics strand

### VC2M1ST01 — Acquire and record data for categorical variables in various ways
*Curriculum (VCAA):* collect and record data for categorical variables (lists, tallies, drawings, symbols), including using digital tools.

**In plain words:** asking a question, recording answers with tallies/lists, and being systematic about it.

**Why it matters next:** Level 2 introduces data displays with more formal conventions; Level 4+ pictographs/columns/median.

**Watch for:** tallying all over the place without groups of five; not deciding categories BEFORE asking; double-counting friendly people who "changed their mind".

**Activities:**
- **Family survey** — favourite pizza topping / ice-cream flavour / weekend activity — they run it, they tally it.
- **Drive-by tally** — count red cars vs other-coloured cars on a 10-minute drive to the shops.
- **Feeling chart** — emoji star chart on the fridge: how each person felt each day this week (elaboration).

**Items:** L1-ST01-1 (best data question) · L1-ST01-2 (tallies in 5s) · L1-ST01-3 (|||| ||| = 8) · L1-ST01-4 (tally vs list vs story) · L1-ST01-5 (observe: family ice-cream survey with tallies)

---

### VC2M1ST02 — Display collected data with one-to-one displays; compare by frequency
*Curriculum (VCAA):* represent categorical data with one-to-one displays and digital tools; compare using frequencies; discuss findings.

**In plain words:** making the picture (one sticker per vote) and answering "which got most?" and "by how many?".

**Why it matters next:** reading pictographs where one symbol = TWO (L2) and bar graphs (L3) build directly on this.

**Watch for:** not matching the display scale to counts; "apple is tallest" without being able to say how many tall; not handling a category with zero votes.

**Activities:**
- **Button graph** — sort 30 buttons by colour; make a one-to-one column of each colour; compare heights.
- **Emoji class chart** — ask each family member 😊/😐/😢 about their week; draw the chart on the fridge.
- **"How many MORE?"** — between any two categories, count the difference aloud.
- **Zero row** — talk about what an empty column means and why you still draw it (elaboration).

**Items:** L1-ST02-1 (😊×5 = 5 votes) · L1-ST02-2 (apples vs bananas) · L1-ST02-3 (count by fives in tallies) · L1-ST02-4 (zero pears column) · L1-ST02-5 (observe: one-to-one display + most popular + difference)

## Scoring & reporting cheat-sheet (app logic)

- Per code: 5 items × 2 points = 10 max. **Secure ≥ 8 · Developing 5–7 · Emerging 0–4**.
- Strand result = mean of code percentages within that strand.
- Overall "readiness toward Level 1 mastery" = mean across all 15 codes (weights all equal).
- Gap report wording: **Strong** (≥80%) → celebrate + extend; **Developing** (50–79%) → practise at home 2×/week; **Needs work** (<50%) → focused at-home block, mention to teacher.
- Proficiency lens: item tags tell us whether it's **fluency** slips (fast wrong answers) vs **reasoning** gaps (can't explain) vs **problem-solving** struggles (can't set up the problem). The report calls this out per code.

## Notes & honest caveats

- This is a **parent-guided check-in, not a validated diagnostic**. It tells you curriculum coverage; it does not replace teacher judgement, and strong results don't guarantee school success.
- Level 1 is roughly Year 1 (ages 6–7). If your child is struggling widely, talk to the teacher — the app will say this too.
